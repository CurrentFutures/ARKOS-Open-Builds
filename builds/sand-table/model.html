The sand table

The agent's computer in a concrete body below; a tray of sand floating 4 cm above it on four brass posts. When a task ends, the plate rings for a moment and the sand jumps into a figure: crisp if the agent was settled, smeared if it was unsettled. The tone you hear is the brass itself ringing. The figure stays until the next task. When it looks right, make the files: cut files for the shops, and the software for the three machines.

What you're seeing

  • Size: about 60 × 60 cm and 31 cm tall. An object for the floor or a sideboard, not a table.
  • Body: glass-fiber reinforced concrete with two compartments behind a perforated brass divider: the agent's small computer on the left, the Mac Studio (the model and the meter) on the right, raised on feet. One cable crosses between them, through one hole.
  • Brass vents on opposite sides: air in on the left, out on the right, straight across both compartments.
  • Gap: 4 cm on four brass posts with rubber isolators. The back-right post is hollow: the one-way wire and the tray's low-voltage power go up it.
  • Sand tray: walnut, with a square brass plate on a driver at its center, edges free, sized by the desk test. Glass microbeads, 100–200 micrometres. A lift-off glass cover.
  • The figure: one figure, chosen by the household at setup from the ones its own plate can make. Clarity is the reading everywhere: crisp is settled, smeared is unsettled.
  • The tone is the plate: no speaker. Unsettled makes it grainy. It rings down like a bell.
  • Front edge: the key (SEALED, ONLINE, ACT), four telltales (working, tool call, web open, may act) and two counters that only count up: tasks, and unsettled tasks.
  • Gap light: steady when on, breathing when resting, a heartbeat blink when off. It can be switched off.
  • Honesty: the agent lives on its own computer and can't reach the model's runtime, the meter or the table. The meter is arithmetic. One wire, one direction, a controller with no radio.
