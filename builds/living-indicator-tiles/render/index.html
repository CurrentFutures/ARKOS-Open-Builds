Living Indicator Tiles
Materials as governance · observable by people and agents

Living indicator tiles

A thin layer of algae, warmed by the habitat's thermal loop, lit from behind. The tiles light a corridor and report on the loop at the same time: how much light gets through shows how much is growing, and the color shows how it's doing. A person reads the color against the reference band along the bottom; an agent reads the same light with a sensor.

Try a tile

Move the loop temperature and the time since the last harvest. Real algae take days to respond; this shows where a tile settles.

Why backlit

The light that lets you read the tile is the light the algae grow on. Every tile runs the same fixed backlight, so differences between tiles come from the loop, not the lamp. The fixed light also makes the agent's reading exact: known light in, measured light out.

Why the reference band

People judge absolute color badly and compare colors well. The band along the bottom of every tile is printed healthy color under the same light, so anyone can see "greener than it should be" or "too pale" without training.

Why it's governance

A culture can't be quietly reconfigured. If an agent's thermal readings say the loop is fine and the tiles have gone yellow, that disagreement is itself the alarm. The tiles are the shared record nobody owns.

Colors are illustrative, modeled from how algal pigments absorb light (chlorophyll absorbs red and blue; the blue pigment phycocyanin absorbs orange-red and breaks down with heat; carotenoids absorb blue-green and build up under stress). Real tiles need calibrating per species, layer thickness and backlight. Agent readings show modeled light transmission at 620 nm (phycocyanin) and 680 nm (chlorophyll), the wavelengths a simple sensor would watch. Temperatures assume a Spirulina-like culture with an optimum around 30–35 °C.
