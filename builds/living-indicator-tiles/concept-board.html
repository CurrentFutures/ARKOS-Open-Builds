Living Light
Concept · materials as governance in space habitats

Living light

Backlit algae tiles warmed by the habitat's thermal loop. They light the corridors, and their color and density show anyone walking past, person or agent, whether the loop is running right. A warm stretch of loop shows up as a yellowing stretch of wall.

02 · Three directions

Where the tiles live

Direction A · start here

Wall panels

A continuous band of tiles at eye level along every corridor, one tile per loop segment. Easiest to read, easiest to plumb, and the whole loop becomes a map you walk along.

Direction B

Floor path

Walkable tiles marking routes. Optional bioluminescent layer that flashes underfoot. Harder: load-bearing glass, cleaning, and the culture needs protecting from impact.

Direction C

Lantern modules

Hanging or standing units, one per room or system, like the media lantern with a living pane. Most sculptural; fewest readings.

03 · Tile anatomy

One tile, front to back

  1. 1Cover glass. Low-iron, so it doesn't tint the reading. Cleanable side.
  2. 2Culture layer, 5–10 mm. Thin enough to stay readable. Its own sealed loop with a harvest port and a gas membrane (CO₂ in, oxygen out).
  3. 3Reference band. Printed healthy color under the same light, along the bottom edge. Plus the harvest date.
  4. 4Heat plate. Carries the loop's heat into the culture without the coolant ever touching it. Algae in a real coolant loop would foul it.
  5. 5Fixed backlight. Same brightness and spectrum on every tile, on a day-night cycle, so differences come from the loop, not the lamp.
  6. 6Optical sensor. Measures light through the culture at the pigment wavelengths. The agent's copy of what people see.
04 · Potential components

What it could be built from

PartCandidatesWhy, and the catch
CultureSpirulina (Arthrospira); a warm/cool species pairSpirulina's blue pigment fades with heat: a built-in thermometer. A pair separates "too hot" from "stressed." Each needs calibrating.
Glow layer (optional)Bioluminescent dinoflagellates (Pyrocystis)Flash when disturbed, so floor tiles light underfoot. Narrow temperature range, needs its own dark cycle.
PanelFlat-panel photobioreactor, glass or acrylicLab and pilot versions exist. Thin, sealed, with ports for harvest and gas.
BacklightFixed-spectrum white LED panelWhite keeps the color honest for people. Grow-light red/blue would distort the reading.
SensorMulti-channel spectral sensor chip (e.g. ams AS7341) or a camera with filtersCheap and small. Reads 620 nm (blue pigment) and 680 nm (chlorophyll).
Heat couplingAluminum or stainless cold plate on the loopShares heat, never water. The key safety separation.
Culture loopPeristaltic pump, membrane gas exchanger, harvest valveMixing matters in low gravity: bubbles don't rise on their own.
ReferenceUV-printed band on the cover glassThe same printing method as the lantern's panes.

Precedents to read: ESA's MELiSSA closed-loop life-support project (uses Spirulina); the BIQ House in Hamburg (an algae bioreactor façade); NASA's OMEGA project (algae in floating membranes).

First prototype

One living pane: a sealed 5 mm algae layer, backlit, on a small heated plate with a dial. Turn the heat up over a week and photograph it daily. That tests the whole idea on a desk, and it could sit in the media lantern as a side experiment.

All colors on this page are modeled from how algal pigments absorb light, not photographed from real cultures.
