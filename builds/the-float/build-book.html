1 · Concept

The Float: a lit cabinet hovering over a walnut block

A gaming and modding PC (heavily modded Skyrim SE, the Creation Kit, new games as they come out, 3D-printer slicing) built as a piece of furniture for a living room. The case sits 4 cm above a solid walnut block on four brass posts. Its curved glass corner is wrapped in one reeded panel, so you see without quite seeing: glowing fan rings and dark shapes, broken into vertical bands. The gap under the case does the breathing and catches the light.

Where it sitsOn the right-hand wing of a fixed L-desk, to the user's right, reeded corner facing the chair, back toward the wall (≥10 cm gap)
SizeAntec C8 Curve Wood, 303 × 464 × 476 mm, on 4 cm posts and a 9 cm walnut base: about 61 cm tall overall
Build pathFull DIY (choosing the graphics card and fans ourselves makes buying a prebuilt pointless)
BuilderA first-time builder, with helpers for woodwork and electronics

What you see

  • The reeded wrap: reeded film over the C8's single curved glass panel, front and left side, reeds vertical. Everything inside becomes soft vertical bands of light and shape.
  • The light: ten programmable fans (three under the graphics card, three on the side, three on the top radiator, one at the back). Gold is the home color; it can be changed any evening. Warm white edge strips add fill.
  • The walnut: the C8's own walnut side panel, and the walnut base.
  • The float: four brass posts lift the case 4 cm. The bottom fans pull air through that gap, and their light spills out of it onto the walnut. The back-right post is a hollow brass tube carrying the switch and lighting wires up into the case, so nothing dangles.
  • The base: a solid walnut block. No holes (it has no airflow job). It holds a brass toggle switch, a hidden "dead-front" power indicator, a brass inlay line with a lit seam beneath it, the controller and the wiring.
  • The back: stock, industrial. The power cord drops straight down from the power supply's socket, past the gap, into a brass clip where the brass line ends.
  • Inside: the Gigabyte Dark Wood motherboard (wood strips, champagne-bronze heatsink), gold memory with a color-adjustable light bar, amber power cables, gold mirror strips on the divider.
  • Power: an orange Conway Extō power box on the floor. Orange marks power outside the case only.

2 · Principles

Design principles

The rule is "every choice deliberate." Every element must be justified by airflow, touch, or a named reference. Use these when swapping a part or improvising, but don't treat them as a cage: if something looks better and still has a reason, it's in.

  1. See without quite seeing. The reeded wrap is the main effect. It turns hardware into light and shape, so parts don't have to be beautiful up close; they have to glow or catch light.
  2. Light first, parts second. Spend on programmable fans and lighting control before decorative parts. Gold is the home color; the ability to change it is a feature.
  3. Hide the light source when you can. Fans glow behind the reeds; strips sit in channels; indicators shine through tiny holes in wood. No bare LEDs on the outside.
  4. Light reports the machine. The seam shows state (on, asleep, off). The rays show effort (watts). Breathing is a nice extra, not a requirement.
  5. Material meanings. Walnut: furniture surfaces. Brass, unlacquered: what your hand and eye follow (toggle, inlay, posts, clip); it darkens with age and the lever stays bright where touched. Gold: a few accents inside. Orange: power outside the case. Amber: power cables inside. Dark metal: everything else.
  6. Airflow is honest. Holes only where air goes. The C8 breathes in through its bottom fans, so the gap does that job and the walnut base stays solid.
  7. Every part earns its brass. The posts carry weight; one carries wires; the clip holds the cord; the toggle switches. No brass for decoration alone.
  8. Industrial where it works. The back is the working face and stays stock. No veneers or skins without a job.
  9. Reversible and serviceable. Films peel, posts unbolt, the base's bottom panel comes off for the electronics, the case's bottom filter slides out. Mains cords are never modified.
  10. It's a fun computer. Performance first where it matters (graphics card, CPU, 64 GB of memory, 4 TB of storage) so new games and side projects just work.

5 · Make

Things we can't buy, and how to make them

Each item lists what it does, materials, steps, and who should help. SVG cut files, the wiring diagram and the code are prepared separately and shared with whoever makes that piece. All measurements come from the real case once it arrives.

5.1 The walnut base

Job: carry the case on its posts, hold the toggle, indicator, seam, controller and wiring. Size: the C8's footprint (≈303 × 464 mm), 9 cm tall. No holes: the gap above it handles airflow.

  1. Frame: 12 mm birch plywood box (bottom plate, top, four sides) with internal blocking under each post position.
  2. Walnut skins: 3 mm walnut plywood on the top and four sides. Each side skin is cut as two horizontal bands with a 6 mm gap at 3.6 cm from the bottom, for the brass line.
  3. Inside: a shelf for the controller, an LED channel behind the seam, a pocket in the front for the ray module, and a wire path from the controller to the back-right post.
  4. Bottom: removable panel on screws or magnets; felt feet.
  5. Finish: hardwax oil on the walnut before sanding the brass flush.

Cut at a makerspace laser or via an online service. Test-cut in MDF first. Tools: clamps, wood glue, two-part epoxy, sandpaper. ≈$100–200 in materials and cutting.

5.2 Brass posts (the float)

  1. Four 4 cm lengths of ~2.2 cm (7/8") brass: three solid round bar, one thick-wall tube for the wires. Face both ends square (a metal shop or a lathe at a makerspace).
  2. Drill and tap each end: the bottom takes a bolt up through the base's blocking; the top takes a bolt through the case's foot mounting holes (remove the C8's stock feet).
  3. The hollow post lines up with a hole in the base top and a grommeted hole in the case floor (rear chamber), so the switch and lighting wires pass straight up.
  4. Small brass washers under each post; leave unlacquered.
  5. Keep the gap clear: nothing on the base top under the case. The bottom filter must still slide out.

5.3 Brass inlay line, lit seam and cord clip

  1. Flat brass bar ≈1.5 × 6 mm, set with epoxy into the 6 mm gap between skin bands, then sanded flush. Path: from the toggle, around the front-left corner, along the left side, around to the back, ending at the cord clip under the power supply's socket.
  2. Directly below the brass, a 1.5 mm reveal is lit from inside by warm LEDs: the seam.
  3. The cord clip is a brass half-ring with two small screwed flanges (a bent brass strip or a small brass pipe clamp works). The cord drops straight down from the socket, past the gap, into the clip.

5.4 The brass toggle and the controller

  • Why a controller: a PC wants a momentary press, but brass toggles latch. A small microcontroller reads the toggle and sends the PC a 0.2 s "press" through an optocoupler (e.g. PC817), keeping the PC electrically separate.
  • Wiring:
    • Toggle → controller input.
    • Controller output → optocoupler → motherboard PWR_SW header, up through the hollow post.
    • Motherboard power-LED header → second optocoupler → controller input, so it knows on / asleep / off.
    • Rays (12 addressable LEDs), seam LEDs and edge strips (via a MOSFET module) → controller.
  • Power: a USB-C charger in the Extō keeps the controller alive while the PC sleeps (so the seam can breathe).
  • Software: a small PC program reads CPU and graphics card power and sends watts over USB; 1 ray per 50 W. The fans and memory lighting are set from the PC (SignalRGB or OpenRGB), default gold.
  • The C8's own power button still works; ignore it or hide it.

Low voltage only. Arduino-level skills.

5.5 Dead-front ray indicator

Thin walnut veneer (~0.6 mm) laser-drilled with ~0.3 mm holes along 12 ray lines around the toggle, over 3 mm opal acrylic and a light-blocking grid (one cell per ray), with the 12 LEDs behind. Drops into the pocket in the base front. Test hole size on scrap veneer.

5.6 The reeded wrap

  • Reeded film on the inside face of the curved glass, reeds running vertically, so the film only bends in one direction around the corner. Apply wet with a felt squeegee, working from the middle of the curve outward.
  • A light bronze tint film under it if you want the warmer veil.
  • Buy extra; the first attempt on a curve may need a redo.

5.7 Fans and lighting setup

  • 3 × 120 mm bottom (intake through the gap), 3 × side (intake), 3 × top on the radiator (exhaust), 1 × rear (exhaust).
  • Daisy-chain lighting and power where the fans allow; connect to the motherboard's 5 V addressable headers or a SATA-powered hub.
  • Default: steady gold. Slow breathe as an option. Set the memory's light bar the same.
  • Warm edge strips in channels along the top and bottom front edges and the back corner, as fill.
  • Gold mirror strips on the divider in front of the motherboard to bounce light.

5.8 Lit cord

The stock power cord (never cut) inside a translucent orange braided sleeve with a side-glow fiber, lit from a small LED at the clip and set by the controller to match the seam.

6 · Safety

Safety and serviceability rules

  • Mains: never cut, splice or re-sleeve a mains cord's insulation. Sleeves go over certified cords.
  • 12V-2x6: push the graphics card's power connector in until it clicks with no gap; check it after the first gaming session.
  • The gap is an air intake: keep it clear; clean the bottom filter every few months.
  • Posts: bolt them through solid blocking; check the case can't tip (the case is taller than wide).
  • Conductive materials: mirror film edges stay off circuit boards and pins.
  • LED voltage: 5 V addressable parts only on 5 V headers.
  • Static: touch the case frame before handling parts.

8 · Helpers

Who helps with what

  • Woodworker or makerspace: the base, the brass inlay channel, test cuts.
  • Metal shop or lathe: facing, drilling and tapping the brass posts.
  • Electronics friend: controller, optocouplers, LEDs, the wire run through the hollow post.
  • Builder: the PC itself, fans and lighting software, film, finishing.
9 · Open

Open decisions

  • Graphics card: wood, gold, or plain dark (the reeds hide most of it; a plain card saves nothing much at current prices but widens availability).
  • Fan line: pick one programmable 120 mm fan line with good reviews and daisy-chaining.
  • C8 Curve top radiator support: confirm before buying the cooler.
  • C8 wood: solid walnut or a finish (check in person or ask Antec).
Appendix

Plan B: the same idea in the Fractal North XL

If the C8 doesn't work out, the build translates to the Fractal Design North XL (walnut slat front, flat glass side, power supply at the bottom). See the North XL model (a separate page; may require access).

  • Reeded flat side instead of the curved wrap.
  • No float: the case sits directly on the base, which breathes for the power supply through vertical slots at the back and the rear half of each side, on the same column grid as the front slats.
  • Walnut slat cap over brass mesh on the top; brass mesh behind the front slats.
  • Lighting: plain fans, light from warm edge strips (or swap to programmable fans).
  • Back: stock or a perforated black steel or brass plate if the stock back is solid.
  • Everything else (toggle, rays, brass line, seam, clip, Extō, board, memory) carries over.
