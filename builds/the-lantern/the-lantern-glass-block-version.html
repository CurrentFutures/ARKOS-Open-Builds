An object for the home first, a media player second. Four glass blocks stand on a concrete base around an open middle; inside, a silent Raspberry Pi plays films to the TV, and light glows out through the rippled glass. Real glass, concrete and felt: nothing to peel, bubble or warp, and every part from a hardware store or a maker's shop.

Principle it applies: dignity in design. A computer treated as furniture: silent, made of honest materials, and easy to repair. The Pi lifts straight out through the top.

How it works

  • Light: glass blocks are sealed and hollow, so the light can't go inside them. Four short LED strips stand in the inside corners and shine out through all four blocks, rippled by the wave pattern. Colour is chosen for the room, not to report anything.
  • Felt: under each block it cushions glass on stone and keeps the joints even; the felt feet lift the base so air can get under it, and stop it scratching furniture.
  • Concrete: weight that keeps a heavy glass tower steady, a hole for air and cables, and the stone that frames the glass.

Try the look first

Before casting anything: stand the four blocks in the pinwheel on a table at night, put an LED strip or a phone's torch in the middle, and look. That's about $30–80, and the blocks go straight into the build if you like it.

Make it

  1. Make the mold. A melamine box 320 × 320 mm inside, 50 mm deep. Glue an 88 mm square XPS block in the centre (the hole), and a 30 × 15 mm XPS strip from it to the back edge on the floor of the mold (the cable channel, under the base). Seal the seams with silicone; coat with release agent.
  2. Cast the base. Mix and pour, following the mix's instructions; vibrate out the bubbles by tapping the mold. Cover and keep it damp for a week. Unmold, dig out the foam, and seal it.
  3. Set up the Pi. Flash LibreELEC for the Raspberry Pi 5 onto the card (libreelec.tv), put the Pi in its passive case, and connect it to the TV to set up Kodi and the library.
  4. Set up the light. Install WLED on the light controller from a browser (kno.wled.ge). Cut four 18 cm pieces of strip, wire them in a chain, and set WLED's current limit to suit the supply (2.5 A for a 3 A supply).
  5. Seat the blocks. Felt feet under the base. A strip of 3 mm felt where each block will stand. Stand the blocks in the pinwheel, End blocks with their finished ends at the corners. Check the middle is square, then run clear silicone down each vertical joint, inside and out. Leave a day.
  6. Fit the inside. Stick the LED strips in the four inside corners, facing out. Lay the perforated plate over the hole, stand the Pi on its short end on the plate, and run every cable down through the hole and out along the channel. The power supplies stay outside, so their heat does too.
  7. Top frame. Bed it on dabs of clear silicone across the block tops. The 100 mm opening stays open: that's where warm air leaves and where the Pi comes out for repair.

Honest limits

  • Not built yet. The heat figures are estimates; the test above is how to find out.
  • Video: the Pi 5 plays 4K HEVC smoothly in hardware, but has no hardware decoding for AV1: 4K AV1 films stutter. Most libraries are HEVC; if yours is AV1, use the full Lantern's computer.
  • Heavy glass: the tower is heavy and the blocks are brittle. Put it on something solid, away from where children climb.
  • Rough ends: standard blocks have rough mortar ends; End blocks hide one per block, the top frame hides the top edges.
