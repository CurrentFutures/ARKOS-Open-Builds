# The Lantern

A home media server you'd want in the living room: a silent, fanless computer and two drives inside a block of tinted glass panes on a cast concrete base.

**Status:** Build book complete; not yet built.
**Principles applied:**
- Dignity in design: the computer is treated as furniture, silent and beautiful, made of honest materials (glass, concrete, felt, brushed metal). It opens from the back so drives can be pulled and parts repaired.

## Files

- [model.html](model.html): **the latest model.** Interactive 3D, plus buttons that make the shop cut files (DXF, for acrylic and aluminium) and the 17 glass print files in the colours you choose.
- [build-book.html](build-book.html): the full build book: parts list with rough costs (November 2026), build steps and the rules that can't change.

Open them in a browser. The model is a render, not a photo.

## In short

- 17 acrylic panes, each 10% darker toward the centre, with a window cut around a fanless mini PC.
- Cast concrete base holding a sound-sealed drive vault, with cables dropping through a channel inside the base.
- One 12 V supply runs everything; light is set by a single knob.
- About 4–6 weeks, mostly waiting on shops and concrete. Roughly $1,650–2,800 in total.

## Desk test

Before printing all 17 panes, cut three spares, print one with the tint and square, and hold it over an LED strip in a dark room. If the glow doesn't read the way the model shows, adjust the tint first.

## Honest limits and safety

- Mains wiring and earth bonds should be checked by someone experienced.
- The drive vault is sealed for sound, so heat must leave through the heat pads and lid. Check drive temperatures during the first long copy (under 50 °C).
- About 13 kg. Lift it by the base, never by the glass.
- Prices are rough and change often.
