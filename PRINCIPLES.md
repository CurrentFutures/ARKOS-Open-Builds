# ARKOS principles

These are guidelines, not rules. A build doesn't have to solve every problem, but it applies at least one of these and says which. Builds are how we test whether the principles hold up.

## 1. Legibility by anyone

A system's state should be readable by a person or a machine, without a manual and without taking the system's word for it.

- **Readable states.** Show the state with physics, chemistry or biology, through any channel that both people and machines can read: light, shape, texture, force, sound, smell, weight, temperature, magnetism.
- **Honesty.** Resets are necessary, but a reset leaves a mark. A record that can be wiped without trace isn't a record.
- **Witness, not informant.** A mark shows *what* happened, not *who* did it. Knowing a hatch was opened is safety; knowing who opened it is surveillance, which is a different job and needs a different tool.

## 2. Dignity in design

- **Respect for limited resources.** Use less, use what's already wasted (heat, offcuts, spent water), and design for repair and reuse.
- **Respect for people and organic materials.** Wood, cork, stone, fungus and living cultures are partners in the design, not decoration.
- **Equal standing for people and agents,** and agency on both sides of the glass.
- **Non-humanoid machines.** Robots and agents take the shape their task needs, not a human shape. That's more efficient and more truthful about what they are, and it discourages people from treating them as human.

## The test we apply to every build

Would an engineer want this even without the ARKOS story? If not, it isn't finished.
