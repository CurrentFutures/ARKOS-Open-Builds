The living light wall

Grow food from waste heat, while monitoring the health of your systems in a way that's beautiful and natural. Flow tiles show whether heat is arriving. Grow tiles show whether it's sustaining life, and give back food and oxygen. Nothing here is a screen.

What you're seeing

  • Wall: twelve 30 × 30 cm tiles on a frame rail with quick-release clips. Any tile lifts out by hand.
  • Arrangement: flow tiles in a band at eye level, grow tiles above and below.
  • Flow tile: the loop water itself runs through a clear winding channel, lit from behind. Moving water bends the light; still water leaves it still. No sensors, no controller.
  • Grow tile: a thin sealed glass panel of living spirulina, warmed by loop water through a plate behind it, breathing the room's air, lit on a day and night cycle.
  • Reference band: printed healthy colour along each grow tile's bottom edge, so anyone can see "greener than it should be" or "too pale."
  • Loop water (copper, left): from the building or habitat's thermal loop, or a home server's waste heat. It runs through the flow tiles and the plates behind the grow tiles, then back to the loop.
  • Culture medium (green, right and below): fresh medium refills the grow tiles after a harvest. Harvest drains into the trough and through the strainer: the algae become food, and the spent medium waters the planter.
  • Air (white, top): room air carries people's exhaled CO₂ into the grow tiles; oxygen-richer air returns through the vent above the wall.
  • Safety: healthy colour is not proof the food is safe. Check a sample before eating. Flow tiles carry loop water only, never culture medium.

Grow colours are modelled from how algal pigments absorb light, as in the living indicator tile study. They are illustrative and need calibrating. Light patterns are drawn, not simulated. A few grow tiles barely change a room's CO₂: they are not an air purifier.
