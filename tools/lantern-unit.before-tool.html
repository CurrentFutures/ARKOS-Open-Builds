What you're seeing

  • Glass: 17 panes, 21 × 22 cm, 6 mm thick, 6 mm gaps, open top. Each pane 10% darker toward the center.
  • Film: a soft-edged square of color on each pane, growing in steps toward the center, where it's just bigger than the window.
  • Window: cut through the middle 7 panes, 15.8 × 15.4 cm, around the computer.
  • Rods: four 5 mm nickel-plated (or chrome) rods with acorn nuts hold the panes as one block.
  • Computer: fanless mini PC, 13 × 13 × 4.5 cm, standing, fins vertical.
  • Base: cast concrete, 21 × 20 × 18 cm, on an inset felt plinth.
  • Top: slotted aluminum plate with LED strips under the pane edges and a grommet for the computer's cables.
  • Back: aluminum plate set flush into a cast pocket with a 1.5 mm shadow gap, on four nickel thumbscrews in cast-in threaded inserts. Laser-cut air slots across the top; panel-mount Ethernet, HDMI and power below.
  • Cables: right-angle plugs point down into a channel cast into the concrete, so the cables drop inside the base's outline and leave under the back edge at floor level.
  • Vault: two 3.5" drives standing in a sled on elastic cords; slides out the back.
  • Power: one slim 12 V supply in the air space under the top plate runs the drives, lights and computer.

How to build it

About 4–6 weeks, mostly waiting on shops and concrete. The glass, metal and base can run at the same time.

  1. Buy the computer first and measure it. The window is the computer plus 13 mm on each side and 24 mm above. The panes, top plate and base all size from it. Check its input voltage before paying: many N100-class boxes take 19 V. If it does, add a 12 V to 19 V step-up converter so the single cord still works.
  2. Make the files from one model. Pane cut file, 17 print files, top plate and back plate. One model keeps the plate slots, pane pitch and rod holes lined up. I can generate these.
  3. Test pane (1 week). Cut 3 spare panes; print one with the black tint and square; hold it over an LED strip in a dark room. Adjust the tint before printing all 17.
  4. Glass (2–3 weeks). Laser cut, anneal, UV print the inward faces, peel, assemble on the rods with a 6 mm spacer between each pane. Nuts snug, not tight.
  5. Metal (1–2 weeks, in parallel). Order the plates, lid and brackets; brush the finish; order the hardware.
  6. Cast the base (2–3 weeks including curing). Build the mold with the vault box, plate pocket, cable channel and stainless threaded inserts in place. Cast, wait about a week before handling, seal. Fit the aluminum plates only after the base has cured and been sealed. It weighs about 9 kg.
  7. Bench-test the electronics. Power supply, fuse, controller, strips and knob on the table. Set the WLED modes. Then mount the power supply and controller under the top plate. Bond the top plate, back plate and vault lid to the inlet's earth pin. Have someone experienced check the mains wiring and the earth bonds.
  8. Drives and software. Build the sled, hang the drives, connect the bridge. Fit the heat pads to the lid. Install LibreELEC (Kodi), set the nightly copy, and confirm the drives spin down when idle. Fit the peak-hold labels and write the install date beside them.
  9. Assemble, bottom to top. Felt plinth, base, drive sled, vault lid with gasket, top plate with electronics (cables through the grommet), back plate and cables, computer, glass block.
  10. Final checks. Play 4K for two hours and check the glass at the top of the window with a cheap probe thermometer: it should stay under 50 °C. Then the vault test, which matters most: over a few days, run the full library copy and a long drive self-test back to back, with the room warmed to about 30 °C to stand in for summer. Watch the drives' own temperature readings; afterwards the labels should show no step past 50 °C. If they do, open the fallback vents and repeat. Listen at 1 m. Run every light mode. Lift the glass off and back on.

Rules that can't change

  • Window clearance: at least 13 mm around the computer's sides and 24 mm above it.
  • Pane pitch is 12 mm (6 mm pane plus 6 mm gap). The top-plate slots and spacer length must match. Keep an odd number of panes so one pane sits at the darkest center.
  • Rod holes 6 mm for 5 mm rods, and don't clamp the stack. Acrylic expands about seven times more than glass.
  • Cast acrylic, annealed after cutting. No alcohol or ammonia cleaners.
  • Print on the inward faces, and leave a 2 cm clear band at each pane's bottom edge.
  • The vault stays sealed from the air space above it. Sound only escapes if you leave a path.
  • Power supply at least 75 W, fused at the inlet. All mains voltage stays inside the inlet and the enclosed power supply.
  • Earth the metal. Top plate, back plate and vault lid bonded to the inlet's earth pin, so a loose wire can never make them live.
  • No aluminum in wet concrete. Cast-in parts are stainless or brass; aluminum goes on after the base has cured and been sealed.
  • Vault under 50 °C, for life. Sealed for sound means no airflow, so heat leaves through the soft heat pads and the aluminum lid. Peak-hold labels, read through the window in the hatch, keep the worst temperature the vault has ever reached, with no software. If a step past 50 °C ever darkens, pull the two vent plugs: the air flowing through the space above draws air through the vault, at the cost of a little more drive noise.
  • Confirm the computer's input voltage before buying anything else. The single-cord design depends on it.
  • Whole object about 13 kg. Lift it by the base, never by the glass.
